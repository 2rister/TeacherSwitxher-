Talk About Remarkable People

Five short lives · the phrases you need to describe them

Read the five lives

The phrases in bold are the ones you have to learn.

1Elizabeth I · 1533–1603
Elizabeth I was born in 1533 and came from a royal family: her father was King Henry VIII. She received a very good education at Hatfield Palace, and by the age of thirteen she spoke several languages. She became queen in 1558 and never married, because she wanted to keep the power herself. Many people say that this influential leader changed the course of English history. She died at the age of 69.

2Horatio Nelson · 1758–1805
Horatio Nelson was born in 1758 and grew up in a small village in England. He joined the navy when he was twelve and took part in many important battles at sea. He lost the sight in one eye, and then he lost his right arm, but he went back to his ship. At Trafalgar in 1805 he made history: the British won, but Nelson died at the age of 47. For many people he is still an inspiring leader.

3John Lennon · 1940–1980
John Lennon was born in 1940 in Liverpool, and his aunt Mimi brought him up. In 1956 he formed his first band, and later that band became the Beatles. The group achieved great success all over the world, and in 1965 they won an award: the Queen made all four of them Members of the Order of the British Empire. Lennon also played an important role in the peace movement, and he is remembered for his songs. He died at the age of 40.

4Princess Diana · 1961–1997
Diana Spencer was born in 1961 and came from an old, rich family – her father was an earl. She did not study at university. In 1981 she married Prince Charles and became a prominent figure all over the world. She visited hospitals, helped people with HIV/AIDS and worked to stop landmines, so she had a strong influence on society. She died at the age of 36, and people remember her for her charity work.

5Isaac Newton · 1642–1727
Isaac Newton was born in 1642 on a farm in England. He studied at university in Cambridge, and there he made an important contribution to science. He made a discovery about light: white light is a mixture of all the colours. Today he is best known for his discovery of gravity, and every school book calls him an outstanding scientist. He died at the age of 84.

Words to know
a figure – an important person that many people know about
outstanding – much better than the others
influential – able to change what other people think or do
to achieve – to get something after working for it
a contribution – something useful that you add to a piece of work
an award – a prize or an honour that somebody gives you
a movement – a group of people who work together for one idea
society – all the people of a country, taken together

6Now your personSix sentences. Every opinion pays with a fact.

Write six sentences about your person. Use a different phrase from tasks 3 and 4 in each one. After every opinion write because and one fact.

  1. He / She was a prominent figure in …
  2. He / She was born in … and grew up …
  3. He / She became famous for …
  4. He / She made an important contribution to …
  5. One of his / her greatest achievements was …
  6. He / She is remembered for …
Before you hand it in Read your six sentences again and find every word that somebody could argue with – great, influential, remarkable, inspiring. Each of them needs a date, a number or an event standing right behind it. If it has none, cross the word out or find the fact.
